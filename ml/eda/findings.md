# OMNIWeb OMNI2 hourly — EDA findings

Generated by `ml/eda.py` against the full curated dataset (561,024 hourly
rows, 1963-01-01 through 2026-09, `s3://space-weather-curated-*/curated/omniweb_omni2_hourly/`).

## Gaps

Zero missing timestamps. The dataset is a complete, regular hourly grid for
the entire 1963-2026 span — NOAA pre-pads incomplete/future rows with fill
sentinels rather than omitting them, and the parser (`scripts/omni2_format.py`)
already converts those sentinels to nulls. So "gaps" in this dataset show up
as per-field nulls, not missing rows.

## Nulls (per-field, i.e. real instrument/coverage gaps)

Highest null rates are in derived/energetic-particle and IMF-vector-quality
fields — largely a pre-modern-instrumentation artifact:

| field | null % | why |
|---|---|---|
| proton_flux_gt2mev / gt4mev | ~65% | energetic particle instruments not flown/returning data for much of the record |
| na_np_ratio, sigma_na_np | ~41% | alpha/proton ratio, needs higher-fidelity plasma instruments |
| plasma_speed, proton_density, IMF components | ~23-26% | early-era (pre-1973ish) spacecraft coverage gaps |
| **kp, dst_index, ap_index, sunspot_number_r** | **~0.5%** | only missing for a handful of not-yet-elapsed hours in 2026 |

The core geomagnetic/solar indices we actually care about for forecasting
(Kp, Dst, sunspot number) are essentially complete (99.5%+) for the entire
64-year span. Solar wind plasma/IMF fields (candidate predictive features)
have real gaps concentrated in the earliest ~10-15 years of the record.

## Outliers

Sanity-checked against physically-plausible ranges; nothing flagged as a
parsing bug:

- `dst_index`: min -589 nT — this is the real March 1989 storm that caused
  the Quebec blackout, not a data error.
- `plasma_speed`: 16 rows below 200 km/s (down to 156 km/s) — real, rare
  slow-solar-wind conditions, not truncation.
- `kp`, `proton_density`, `field_magnitude_avg`: all within expected bounds.

## Seasonal / solar-cycle effects

- **Solar cycle**: yearly-mean sunspot number clearly shows the ~11-year
  cycle (peaks near 1979, 1989, 2000, 2014, 2024). Kp/Dst track it loosely
  but noisily at yearly resolution — expected, since storms are driven by
  transient CME/CIR events layered on top of the slower cycle, not the
  cycle phase alone.
- **Semiannual (Russell-McPherron) effect**: confirmed in the monthly
  climatology — Kp is elevated and Dst more negative around the equinoxes
  (Mar/Apr, Sep/Oct) versus the solstices (Jun/Jul, Dec/Jan). This is a
  well-documented real geomagnetic effect (Earth-Sun geometry increasing
  coupling efficiency near equinox), not noise — good confirmation the
  dataset and index derivation are trustworthy.

Plots: `solar_cycle_vs_geomagnetic_activity.png`, `seasonal_climatology.png`.

## Implication for modeling

- Kp/Dst/sunspot number are reliable targets/features across nearly the
  full historical record.
- Solar wind plasma/IMF features (the physically-motivated predictors for
  a Kp forecast) are much sparser before ~1973-1995 depending on field —
  training windows should likely start from a year where those fields are
  consistently populated, rather than using the full 1963+ range.
